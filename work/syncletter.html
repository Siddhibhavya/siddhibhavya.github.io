Building Syncletter

Workplace messages can be hard to interpret.

Our aim was to make workplace communication feel clearer and less stressful while keeping the user in control.

In past internships, I noticed workplace messages are hard to interpret without shared context. Jargon and brief replies leave new employees unsure what a colleague means, or how urgently to respond.

Syncletter decodes a confusing phrase on tap: what it likely means, how urgent it is, and a few reply pointers. Everything stays on your device, and the sender never knows.

How might we help early-career employees understand ambiguous workplace messages and respond with more confidence, while respecting their privacy and keeping them in control?

Research narrowed a broad question about digital misunderstandings to one moment: the receiver trying to interpret a message from someone they do not know well.

  • 6/10 already reread and second-guess before sending!
  • 5/10 users agree corporate tone-monitoring isn't just effortful; it's described as exhausting and constant.
  • 8/10 users want to keep the choice to stay "human," even when they know a suggestion might be better.
  • A full AI rewrite is trusted less than a human's own imperfect wording; this came up independently and unprompted in every second interview!

I am fairly new to the corporate world, and honestly I do not understand the overly formal mails my HR sends.

I observed how opinions fell along a spectrum:

I learnt I wasn't the only one struggling!

– Participant, age 22, from Bangalore.

Looking at the current market, we found many tools that offered full rewrites and paraphrasing, and that were standalone apps.

Here is where Syncletter differs.

It is local and editable, and sits within the texting app.

Logo/Icon

Expected Experience on WhatsApp

It runs on a personal glossary you teach it: meanings are saved per sender and can be changed anytime.

Reply cues give direction, not ready-made sentences, so you keep your voice. Privacy comes first: the sender is never notified and everything is handled on-device.

Why a PWA?

A PWA lets two people text each other in a shared room, so I could test the decode-and-reply flow and get real feedback.

It starts with an inbuilt, editable library of the 200 most common jargons and idioms, to help any new joinee settle in.

Design Decisions & Future Scope

Decision

No AI implementation

DecisionBuild a Context Library.

WhyInterviews raised concerns about message privacy and AI replies sounding unlike the user. Syncletter supports learning workplace language while keeping users in control of their words.

Trade-offSaved meanings are easy to review and edit, but coverage depends on what the library contains.

Direction

Scalability

NowA standalone PWA for phrase lookup and reply cues.

NextExpand the library, support sender-specific meanings, and explore access from messaging apps.

Future considerationAI could handle more varied messages, but would need further evaluation of context, accuracy, privacy, and cost.

Reflection

I learnt to narrow a broad communication problem into a specific user need. I began with tone and emotional misreading, and research brought the focus to the receiver's doubt about workplace phrases and urgency.
